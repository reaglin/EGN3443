Introduction

When engineers collect data — test results, failure times, sensor readings, survey responses — the first question is usually "what is a typical value?" Measures of central tendency answer that question. The mean is the arithmetic average, the median is the middle value once the data are ordered, and the mode is the value that occurs most often. Each summarizes the "center" of a dataset, but each does so differently, and they can disagree sharply.

This topic matters because almost everything that follows in the course builds on it. Variance and standard deviation are measured around the mean; the shape of a distribution is often described by how the mean, median, and mode relate to one another; and later work in probability and inference depends on knowing what a sample average represents. Just as importantly, choosing the wrong measure can distort a conclusion — a single extreme value can pull the mean far from anything typical, while the median barely moves.

As you watch, focus on three things: the exact computational procedure for each measure (especially the median with an even number of observations), how outliers and skew affect each measure differently, and the decision rules for choosing one measure over another based on data type (nominal, ordinal, interval/ratio) and distribution shape. Try pausing each video to compute the answer yourself before the presenter reveals it.
