Introduction

Probability is the mathematical language we use to describe uncertainty. In engineering, almost nothing is known with perfect certainty: material strengths vary, sensors drift, components fail at unpredictable times, and measurements carry error. Probability gives us a disciplined way to quantify that uncertainty on a fixed scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain), so that vague statements like "this part will probably hold" can be replaced by numbers that support design decisions and risk analysis.

This week you are building the vocabulary that the rest of the course depends on. Terms such as experiment, outcome, sample space, and event have precise technical meanings, and the rules you will learn later (complement, addition, multiplication) are stated in that vocabulary. You will also meet the distinction between theoretical probability, derived from the structure of the experiment, and experimental (empirical) probability, estimated from observed data — a distinction that matters any time you compare a model to real test results.

As you watch the videos below, keep a running glossary. Note how each presenter defines the sample space and how they list or diagram outcomes, watch for how probabilities are interpreted in words rather than just computed, and pay attention to any discussion of relative frequency and what happens as the number of trials grows. Where the videos overlap, compare notation; where they differ in emphasis, ask yourself which framing is most useful for an engineering problem.
