EGN3443 Prob and Stats for Engineers

This course applies mathematical and scientific principles to solve a wide variety of practical problems in Engineering Professions. It contains probability and statistical analysis for these problems and develops related Engineering graphs, charts, and measurements.

    Week 2/Module 1 - Statistics for Engineers

    This module introduces engineers to the foundational statistical concepts essential for data-driven decision-making in technical environments. Learners will explore key topics such as descriptive statistics, probability distributions, and data variability, examining how these principles apply to real-world engineering problems. By the end of the module, participants will be equipped to interpret statistical data accurately and apply analytical techniques to support engineering analysis and quality control.

    Week 3/Module 2 - Descriptive Statistics

    In this module, learners explore the core concepts of descriptive statistics, including measures of central tendency, variability, and data distribution. Through practical examples and exercises, learners develop the ability to summarise and interpret datasets meaningfully. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to select and apply appropriate statistical measures to communicate key insights from data clearly and accurately.

    Week 4/Module 3 Probability Concepts

    This module introduces foundational probability concepts, guiding learners through key principles such as sample spaces, events, and the rules governing likelihood. Learners will explore both theoretical and experimental probability, developing the ability to calculate and interpret probabilities in a variety of real-world contexts. By the end of the module, participants will be equipped to apply core probability rules confidently and accurately in data analysis and decision-making scenarios.

    Week 5/Module 4 - Discrete Probability Distributions

    In this module, learners explore the foundational concepts of discrete probability distributions, examining how probabilities are assigned to distinct outcomes in real-world scenarios. Through guided examples and applied exercises, participants will develop the ability to identify, interpret, and work with key distributions such as the binomial and Poisson distributions. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to calculate probabilities, determine expected values and variances, and apply these techniques to solve practical problems in their field.

    Week 6/Module 5 - Continuous Probability Distributions.

    In this module, learners explore the key characteristics and applications of continuous probability distributions, with a focus on the normal, exponential, and uniform distributions. Through worked examples and practical exercises, participants develop the ability to calculate probabilities, interpret distribution parameters, and apply these concepts to real-world scenarios. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to select and use appropriate continuous distributions to model and analyse data in professional and research contexts.

    Week 7/Module 6 - Sampling Distrbutions

    In this module, learners explore the concept of sampling distributions and how they form the foundation for statistical inference. Through guided examples and practice, participants examine the behavior of sample statistics, the Central Limit Theorem, and how sample size influences variability. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to interpret sampling distributions and apply this understanding to real-world data analysis scenarios.

  • Module 8

    Week 8/Module 7 - Point Estimation and CI

    In this module, learners explore the fundamental concepts of point estimation and confidence intervals, examining how sample statistics are used to estimate unknown population parameters. Through applied examples and guided exercises, learners develop the ability to construct and interpret confidence intervals for means and proportions under varying conditions. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to critically evaluate the precision and reliability of estimates in real-world data analysis contexts.

  • Module 9

    Week 9/Module 8 - Hypothesis Testing

    In this module, learners explore the fundamental principles of hypothesis testing, including how to formulate null and alternative hypotheses, select appropriate test statistics, and interpret p-values and significance levels. Through practical examples and guided exercises, learners develop the ability to apply common hypothesis tests to real-world data and draw statistically sound conclusions. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to critically evaluate claims, identify potential errors in testing, and communicate findings with confidence and precision.

  • Module 10

    Week 10/Module 9 - Hypothesis Testing II

    Building on foundational concepts in hypothesis testing, this module guides learners through more advanced inferential techniques, including two-sample tests, paired comparisons, and an introduction to non-parametric methods. Learners will develop the skills to select appropriate tests based on data type and research context, interpret results with confidence, and understand the conditions under which each method is valid. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to apply these techniques to real-world datasets and draw statistically sound conclusions.

  • Module 11

    Week 11/Module 10 - 2 Sample Hypothesis Testing

    This module introduces learners to two-sample hypothesis testing, exploring how to compare means or proportions across two independent or paired groups. Learners will examine the underlying assumptions, select appropriate test statistics, and interpret results within real-world contexts. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to confidently design and execute two-sample tests to support data-driven decision making.

  • Module 12

    Week 12/Module 11 - Regression Analysis

    In this module, learners explore the foundations of regression analysis, examining how to model and quantify relationships between variables to support data-driven decision-making. Through practical application, learners develop skills in building, interpreting, and evaluating both simple and multiple regression models. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to apply regression techniques to real-world datasets and draw meaningful conclusions from their results.

    16 items
  • Module 13

    Week 13/Module 12 - Multiple Regression

    In this module, learners explore multiple regression analysis as a powerful extension of simple linear regression, examining how two or more predictor variables can be used to model and explain variation in a continuous outcome. Learners will investigate key concepts including model specification, interpretation of regression coefficients, and the assessment of model fit using statistical measures such as R-squared and adjusted R-squared. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to construct, evaluate, and interpret multiple regression models using real-world data, strengthening their ability to draw meaningful conclusions from complex datasets.

    14 items
  • Module 14

    Week 14/Module 13 - Validating Regression

    This module guides learners through the critical process of validating regression models to ensure their reliability and generalizability. Learners will explore key validation techniques, including residual analysis, cross-validation, and the assessment of model assumptions such as linearity, homoscedasticity, and independence. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to critically evaluate regression outputs, identify potential model weaknesses, and make informed decisions about model refinement and application.

    16 items
  • Module 15

    Week 15/Module 14 - ANOVA

    In this module, learners explore Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) as a statistical method for comparing means across three or more groups. Through theoretical grounding and practical application, participants examine the assumptions underlying ANOVA, interpret F-statistics, and distinguish between one-way and multi-factor designs. By the end of the module, learners will be equipped to determine when ANOVA is the appropriate test, conduct the analysis, and draw meaningful conclusions from their results.
